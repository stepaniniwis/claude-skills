# Saturated Corpus Results — multi-database sweep (2026-07-18)

**Corpus:** 223 coded rows; **218 in-sample** (screening A or B, problem-coded). 153 clear-include (A), 65 uncertain (B, flagged for full-text). Sources: Scite + Consensus + Scholar Gateway. Six parallel saturation agents (platform, corporate, family, transitional, academic, cross-theory), each running 6–15 query formulations until new includable hits tailed off. Still title/abstract-stage; still no WoS/Scopus/EBSCO (needs institutional access) — so this is a **saturation-of-angles** sample, not yet a defensible PRISMA count. But the pattern below is now stable across a ~2.4× larger corpus and robust to journal quality.

## RQ1 — Problem × context locus (n=218)

| Locus | n | Dominant problem | Share | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Independent | 91 | PERSISTENCE | 30% | **diffuse** — ACTION 24, PERSISTENCE 27, AUDIENCE 15, VALUES 10; no problem >30% |
| Academic | 25 | **HIERARCHY** | 84% | near-pure |
| Platform | 33 | **AUDIENCE** | 67% | + PROTOTYPE 7 |
| Family | 37 | **VALUES** | 46% | + HIERARCHY 15 |
| Social | 14 | **VALUES** | 50% | + HIERARCHY 3 |
| Corporate | 11 | PROTOTYPE | 36% | thin; mixed with PERSISTENCE/HIERARCHY |

**Robustness (AJG 3+/4/4* only):** pattern holds — academic→HIERARCHY 77%, platform→AUDIENCE 67%, family→VALUES 56%, social→VALUES 57%, independent→ACTION 30% (still diffuse). The divergence is **not** an artifact of lower-ranked outlets, which was the pre-registered worry (§ protocol quality criteria).

**Read:** Each *non-default* context cell concentrates on a distinct focal identity problem; the historic default (independent × exclusive) is the one internally diffuse cell — precisely because "entrepreneur" is treated there as an unproblematic category, so every kind of identity question gets asked. Two cells (family, social) share VALUES as dominant → an adjacency that signals **B (family of constructs)** rather than pure **C (umbrella)**.

## RQ2 — Theory × locus (n=218)

- **IDWORK** is the cross-context lingua franca (independent 31, platform 27, family 18, academic 9) — but as a *meta-approach*, its ubiquity is not evidence of construct coherence.
- Substantive theories cluster by cell: **RIT** in independent+academic (role primacy), **IMPR** in platform (17) + independent (signaling), **FIT** confined to independent (12) + social (4), **CONFLICT** in academic (5). No single substantive theory spans the field.

## RQ3 — Core divergence pair confirmed at scale

Same nominal construct ("hybrid entrepreneurial identity"), opposite outcomes by context:

| Hybrid cell | Integrates (S) | Conditional (C) | Fails (F) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Academic** | 6 | 9 | **0** |
| **Corporate** | 1 | 0 | **2** |

Academic hybrids integrate or conditionally manage the dual identity (Jain, Bousfiha, Lam, Wang; zero outright failure); corporate/intrapreneur hybrids predominantly **fail to stabilize** (Starmann's "identity illusion," oscillation). This is the sharpest single piece of divergence evidence in the corpus.

## ⭐ Convergence test at scale — construct portability ≠ problem equivalence

The Fauchart-Gruber founder-identity-types (FIT) construct — the field's strongest candidate for a *coherent, portable* EI construct — **does travel** (18 studies: independent 12, social 4, family 1, multiple 1). Crucially, **Sieger, Gruber, Fauchart & Zellweger (2016, JBV) validated the 15-item scale across 14 countries** — hard convergence evidence a naive umbrella claim cannot survive.

But the *problem* the construct is mobilized to solve still differs by context:
- Independent: FIT → **ACTION** (11/12) — how identity drives effectuation/strategy
- Social: FIT → **VALUES / HIERARCHY** — how identity resolves social-commercial tension

**The construct is portable; the problem is not.** This is the review's central, referee-proof move: it concedes the strongest counterexample (a measurable, cross-nationally validated identity typology) and shows that even a portable instrument answers a *different identity question* in each context. It is what separates conclusion B (shared instruments = a family) from C (distinct problems = an umbrella): **the field is a family at the level of instruments and an umbrella at the level of problems.**

## RQ4 — status against pre-registered rules (§4.5)

- **Rule A (coherent, context moderates): FAILS.** No problem is primary in ≥5/6 cells; each non-default cell has a *different* dominant problem.
- **Rule C (umbrella): partially met** — no problem primary in >2 cells (VALUES is dominant in exactly 2: family+social), and the core RQ3 pair diverges. But the family/social VALUES adjacency and FIT's cross-context portability pull toward **B**.
- **Working conclusion (to be confirmed at full-text): a B/C hybrid** — best stated as the portability/problem distinction above rather than forced into one label. This is a *stronger* contribution than a flat "umbrella" verdict.

## Saturation notes
- Corporate/intrapreneur is **genuinely thin** in the literature (n=11 after hard search across 3 databases) — this is itself a finding: the corporate identity cell is under-studied, which is where the PhD's hybrid focus can contribute.
- Platform and family cells reached clear saturation (agents' final queries returned mostly already-captured DOIs).
- 65 B-flagged papers need full-text adjudication; the biggest B-clusters are (a) family-cell papers where identity is discursive/collective rather than individual (C1 boundary), and (b) intention/aspiration studies at the C4 boundary.
- One explicit counter-voice to log and engage: Cesinger et al. (2021) claims "multiple yet **coherent**" identities among sustainable entrepreneurs — the strongest in-corpus argument against divergence.
