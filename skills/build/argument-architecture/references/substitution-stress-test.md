# Substitution Stress Test

A pre-submission diagnostic for new theoretical concepts. Answers the question 
an editor asks at desk-reject: "Can I explain this with what we already have?"

## Why This Exists

CARS identifies the gap. Toulmin structures the claim. Mulcahy checks the 
contribution level. None of them simulate the editorial substitution reflex: 
the moment when a busy editor reads your new concept and mentally maps it 
onto three existing ones to see if anything is left over.

The Substitution Stress Test makes that reflex explicit and forces you to 
survive it before submission.

## When to Run

Run this test on every paper that introduces a new concept, mechanism, or 
theoretical term. Run it after CARS positioning and Toulmin structuring are 
complete, and before submission. It is especially critical for:

- Papers targeting competitive special issues (high rejection volume)
- Papers introducing neologisms (new theoretical terms)
- Papers claiming "beyond X" positioning (beyond decoupling, beyond conflict, etc.)

## The Three-Step Test

### Step 1: Identify the Three Nearest Neighbours

For your core concept, name the three existing theoretical constructs that a 
knowledgeable editor would reach for as substitutes. These are not the theories 
you cite — they are the theories the editor thinks might already explain your 
finding.

Selection criteria for neighbours:
- **Conceptual proximity**: Does it describe a similar phenomenon?
- **Mechanistic overlap**: Does it invoke a similar causal logic?
- **Empirical coverage**: Could it explain similar data patterns?

For each neighbour, state in one sentence what it explains and how.

### Step 2: Map the Explanatory Coverage

For each neighbour, specify:

**What it explains in your data**: Which empirical patterns in your findings 
could this existing concept account for? Be honest. If Espeland & Sauder's 
reactivity already explains why actors orient toward measurable outputs, 
say so.

**What it does not explain**: Which specific empirical configuration in your 
findings does this concept fail to capture? Be precise. Not "it doesn't fully 
explain" but "it cannot account for [specific pattern X] because [specific 
reason Y]."

**The structural gap**: What is it about the existing concept's architecture 
(its assumptions, scope, causal logic) that prevents it from reaching your 
finding?

### Step 3: Identify the Residual Configuration

After mapping all three neighbours, specify the residual — the empirical 
configuration that none of the existing concepts explains.

Format:

> **Residual configuration**: [Description of the specific empirical pattern 
> that survives substitution]
>
> **Why it survives**: [The structural reason no existing concept covers it — 
> stated in terms of assumptions, scope, or causal architecture, not empirical 
> detail]
>
> **What the new concept adds**: [The specific explanatory work the new concept 
> does that the residual requires]

## Verdict Criteria

| Outcome | Verdict | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Residual is empty or trivial | Concept is substituable | Do not submit. Reframe as extension of existing theory. |
| Residual exists but is narrow | Concept has marginal added value | Sharpen. Make the residual explicit in the Introduction. Consider whether an extension of an existing concept might serve better than a neologism. |
| Residual is substantial and structurally grounded | Concept survives | Proceed. Build the paper's positioning around the residual. |

## Output Format

The stress test produces a structured document with:

1. **Concept under test**: [Name and one-sentence definition]
2. **Neighbour 1**: [Name] — explains [X], fails on [Y] because [Z]
3. **Neighbour 2**: [Name] — explains [X], fails on [Y] because [Z]
4. **Neighbour 3**: [Name] — explains [X], fails on [Y] because [Z]
5. **Residual configuration**: [The surviving pattern]
6. **Verdict**: Substituable / Marginal / Survives
7. **Positioning implication**: [How to rewrite Move 2 of CARS based on findings]

## Common Failure Modes

**Straw-neighbour selection**: Choosing weak alternatives to make your concept 
look good. The test only works if neighbours are strong. Select the concepts 
a senior scholar in your field would actually reach for.

**"My concept is different because it's mine" reasoning**: Restating your 
definition is not a demonstration of non-substitutability. The test requires 
showing an empirical pattern that existing concepts structurally cannot explain.

**Enumerative differentiation**: Listing how your concept differs from each 
neighbour without identifying the shared residual they all miss. The 
residual is the point, not the list.

**The "both/and" trap**: Claiming your concept "combines insights from X and Y" 
without showing that combination produces explanatory value neither achieves 
alone. Synthesis is a valid contribution type (see contribution-types.md), 
but it must earn its keep through residual identification, not through 
additive listing.

## Integration with CARS

The Substitution Stress Test directly feeds Move 2 (Establish Niche). 
A strong niche is not "no one has studied X." It is: "existing concepts A, 
B, and C each explain part of the pattern, but the configuration [RESIDUAL] 
requires a concept that [DOES WHAT YOURS DOES]."

This reframes Move 2 from gap-claiming to residual-demonstrating. The gap 
is not empty space in the literature. It is a specific empirical configuration 
that existing theoretical architecture cannot reach.

## Worked Example: Convergent Neglect

**Concept under test**: Convergent neglect — a field-level mechanism in which 
multiple logics align on a shared strategic end while independently leaving 
the organisational means outside their evaluative scope.

**Neighbour 1**: Selective attention (Ocasio, 1997)
- Explains: Why actors orient toward some issues over others
- Fails on: The specific pattern where *all* logics converge on the same 
  end yet *each* independently neglects the same means. Ocasio's framework 
  does not theorise multi-logic convergence as a source of blind spots; 
  it explains attention allocation within a single attentional structure.
- Structural gap: Ocasio works within organisations; the MDO pattern operates 
  at field level across organisations. The attention is not misallocated within 
  one logic — it is structurally unallocated across all logics simultaneously.

**Neighbour 2**: Decoupling (Meyer & Rowan, 1977)
- Explains: Why formal structures may not match actual practice
- Fails on: Convergent neglect is not about ceremony vs. practice within 
  organisations. It is a field-level pattern where no actor is decoupling — 
  each is genuinely pursuing integration through its own evaluative lens. 
  The neglect is not strategic; it is structural.
- Structural gap: Decoupling requires an actor that maintains a gap between 
  talk and action. Convergent neglect involves no such actor — the gap 
  emerges between actors, not within them.

**Neighbour 3**: Collective action failure (Olson, 1965)
- Explains: Why rational actors may under-provide public goods
- Fails on: The MDO governance gap is not about contribution incentives. 
  Actors do not free-ride on governance; they do not recognise it as their 
  deliverable. The problem is not shirking but categorical non-ownership 
  across evaluative systems.
- Structural gap: Olson assumes actors recognise the good but calculate 
  against contributing. Convergent neglect operates one step earlier: no 
  logic absorbs governance as a primary performance object in the first place.

**Residual configuration**: A field-level pattern in which multiple 
institutional logics converge on a strategic end, each has independent 
evaluative reasons for not owning the organisational means, and the 
aggregate effect is stable non-ownership — not contested ownership, not 
free-riding, not ceremony, but structural orphaning through convergence.

**Verdict**: Survives. The residual is substantial and structurally grounded.

**Positioning implication**: Move 2 should not list what convergent neglect 
is NOT (not decoupling, not collective action). It should demonstrate the 
residual configuration that none of the existing concepts can reach, and 
show why their structural assumptions prevent them from reaching it.
